Correct AI Usage

In my view, I find AI to be a valuable study resource only when it supports learning rather than replaces it. Submitting work completely written by AI defeats the purpose of education and violates the Aggie Honor Code. Additionally, it is also unfair to classmates who put in the time and effort themselves to succeed. All students should use AI to gain an understanding of difficult material, rather than to take shortcuts. 

AI Evaluation Response (Google Gemini):

they build understanding rather than replace original effort."

Benefits of AI

  1. Accelerates mastery of complex course concepts and technical material
  2. Provides real-time feedback when drafting study outlines or reviewing notes

Who Benefits?

  1. Students seeking accessible tutoring and study support resources
  2. Instructors streamlining administrative tasks and lecture scaffolding

How AI Tools Used

  • Structuring preliminary research topics and project outlines
  • Summarizing lengthy academic articles to extract key arguments

Incorrect AI Usage

Figure 1. Glowing Artificial Intelligence Neural Network Brain. Generated by Google Gemini, 20 Oct. 2026.


Common Forms of Academic Misuse:

Unauthorized Submission
Pasting AI-generated essays directly into assignments without personal contribution.
Exam Misconduct
Using generative AI tools to generate answers during online tests or quizzes.
Over-reliance
Bypassing critical reading and relying entirely on AI to solve complex homework problems.

In my opinion, using AI as an outline generator is beneficial, but letting it generate complete work damages true learning. Academic success requires original effort, and turning in AI content directly violates the Aggie Honor Code at Texas A&M. Students must actively evaluate AI responses with critical thinking to ensure academic integrity is always maintained.
